## 1. Zero-shot accuracy: G-LRD Pareto-dominates M-LRD, at 60 % of the paper's recovery

Our five-task averages (plain `acc`, the rendered `avg (5)` column) and the gap to stock:

| row | KV B/token/layer/chip | avg (5) acc | gap to stock | avg (5) paper protocol | gap, paper protocol |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | 1088 | 65.44 | — | 67.27 | — |
| `palu_full_50` (M-LRD 0.5) | 574 | 55.84 | **-9.60** | 58.88 | **-8.38** |
| `glrd_50` (G-LRD 0.5) | **543** | **59.48** | **-5.95** | 62.18 | **-5.09** |
| `palu_full_70` (M-LRD 0.7) | 761 | 61.50 | -3.94 | 63.86 | -3.41 |
| `glrd_70` (G-LRD 0.7) | 759 | **64.09** | **-1.34** | 66.12 | **-1.15** |

"Paper protocol" is the same five tasks scored the way the paper's appendix says it scores them —
`acc_norm` for OpenBookQA and ARC-Challenge, `acc` for PIQA, ARC-Easy and WinoGrande. Both columns are
given because the rendered table's `avg (5)` is plain `acc`; the two orderings are identical and the
gaps differ by at most 1.2 points, so nothing below depends on the choice.

- **Both M-LRD rows are Pareto-dominated by a G-LRD row.** `glrd_50` scores **3.64 points higher than
  `palu_full_50` while storing 5.4 % fewer KV bytes** (543 vs 574), and `glrd_70` scores **2.59 points
  higher than `palu_full_70` at the same bytes** (759 vs 761 — a 0.3 % difference). Nothing in the
  study is a cleaner controlled comparison than the second one: identical memory, identical kernel,
  identical calibration recipe, one knob changed (group size 1 -> 4), +2.59 accuracy and -0.81
  perplexity. Grouping the four KV heads of a chip is free memory-wise and simply better.
- **`glrd_70` is within 1.34 points of the stock model** (64.09 vs 65.44; 1.15 on the paper protocol)
  at a 1.43x KV reduction — a smaller degradation than the paper's *J-LRD* at 50 % (-1.64), though at
  a milder compression ratio, and it is the only compressed row here that beats stock on any task
  (OpenBookQA `acc` 36.8 vs 36.4, +0.40).
- **We reproduce the paper's M-LRD result almost exactly and its G-LRD result only partly.** Per task,
  gap to the row's own baseline, paper protocol against the paper's Table 1:

| method / task | OBQA | PIQA | ARC-e | ARC-c | WinoGrande | avg (5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| paper M-LRD | -4.40 | -4.95 | -11.83 | -14.25 | -9.39 | **-8.96** |
| ours `palu_full_50` | -6.00 | -5.39 | -10.02 | -10.32 | -10.18 | **-8.38** |
| *difference (ours - paper)* | -1.60 | -0.44 | **+1.81** | **+3.93** | -0.79 | **+0.58** |
| paper G-LRD (gs 4) | -0.60 | -2.56 | -5.31 | -7.84 | +0.48 | **-3.17** |
| ours `glrd_50` | -2.80 | -4.03 | -7.66 | -7.17 | -3.79 | **-5.09** |
| *difference (ours - paper)* | -2.20 | -1.47 | -2.35 | **+0.67** | **-4.27** | **-1.92** |

  Our M-LRD row lands **0.58 points better** than the paper's on the five-task average, with the
  per-task pattern matching well (both are worst on ARC-Challenge and ARC-Easy, both lose 9-10 points
  on WinoGrande); the two largest cell differences are in *our* favour. Our G-LRD row lands **1.92
  points worse** than the paper's, and the divergence is concentrated in **WinoGrande (-3.79 for us,
  +0.48 for them, a 4.27-point spread)** and secondarily OpenBookQA and ARC-Easy; on ARC-Challenge we
  are actually 0.67 better.
- **Consequently G-LRD recovers a smaller share of M-LRD's loss here than in the paper: 39 % against
  65 %.** Ours: the gap moves from -8.38 to -5.09, i.e. 39.3 % of M-LRD's loss recovered (38.0 % on
  the plain-`acc` column, -9.60 -> -5.95). The paper's: -8.96 -> -3.17, 64.6 % recovered. **The
  perplexity rows say the same thing to within a point of a percent** — G-LRD removes 39 % of M-LRD's
  perplexity excess over stock for us (§2) against 55 % in the paper — which is a strong internal
  consistency check: two independent measurements, one intrinsic and one task-based, agree on where we
  land relative to the paper.

**Why the G-LRD-specific shortfall, when M-LRD reproduces?** The three known differences (spec §8,
§6 below) do not weigh equally here, and the fact that only the *grouped* row diverges is the clue:

1. **The 32-sample Fisher calibration is the leading suspect, and it should hurt G-LRD more than
   M-LRD.** G-LRD's whitening estimates a 512x512 second-moment matrix per group where M-LRD estimates
   128x128 per head — 16x the parameters from the same 32 calibration sequences — and its rank search
   chooses over a far larger space (one rank per group in 32..512, against one per head in 32..128).
   Both the conditioning of the whitening transform and the reliability of the Fisher rank allocation
   degrade with dimension at fixed sample count, so an under-sampled calibration costs a grouped
   decomposition more than a per-head one. That is exactly the asymmetry we observe, and the paper's
   G-LRD is calibrated with 64x our sample count. This remains a hypothesis: spec §5's optional
   256-sample sensitivity run was not done (§7), so it is untested here.
2. **Llama-3.1 rather than Llama-3.** G-LRD's whole premise is redundancy *between* the KV heads of a
   group; how much of it exists is a property of the checkpoint, and the two revisions differ in
   post-training and in RoPE scaling. A revision with less inter-head redundancy would penalise G-LRD
   specifically and leave M-LRD untouched — again the asymmetry we see. Our stock model is the
   stronger of the two on every comparable measure (wikitext-2 7.254 against 8.28; five-task
   paper-protocol average 67.27 against 66.43), so it also has more to lose.
3. **Device numerics (bf16 activations, bfp8 weights and caches, TP=2) are the weakest explanation for
   this particular gap**, though they are real: they apply equally to the M-LRD rows, which reproduce.
   Where they should bite hardest is the G-LRD reconstruction dot products, which are 4-16x longer
   than M-LRD's (a 404-wide mean V latent against 102), so bfp8 cache quantisation error accumulates
   over a longer sum. Stage 1 bounded the whole device-vs-CPU-fp32 effect at ~1.4 % of perplexity for
   the stock row and 1.3 % for `palu_full_50`; it was not re-measured for the G-LRD rows (§7), so the
   size of this term on the grouped path is an assumption rather than a bound.

A fourth, non-explanation worth stating: this is not a task-harness artefact. Our stock row (ARC-e
82.3, PIQA 80.2, WinoGrande 74.4, OBQA `acc_norm` 44.6, ARC-c `acc_norm` 54.8) sits **0.84 points
above the paper's Llama-3 baseline** on the five-task paper-protocol average and is ahead of it on
four of the five tasks, so the harness, the datasets and the device scoring path are healthy; whatever
costs `glrd_50` its extra 1.92 points is in the decomposition, not in the measurement.

## 2. Perplexity and memory: G-LRD is strictly better than M-LRD at both ratios

At the same nominal compression ratio, G-LRD is **cheaper in KV bytes and better in perplexity than
M-LRD on both rows** — it is not a quality-for-memory trade at all:

| row | r_k (per chip) | r_v (per chip) | ppl (device, 141 windows) | x stock ppl | KV B/token/layer/chip | KV reduction | max ctx @ batch 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | 128 x 4 heads | 128 x 4 heads | **7.254** | 1.00x | 1088 | 1.00x | 28672 |
| `palu_full_50` (M-LRD) | 32-64 (mean 33) x 4 | 64-128 (mean 102) x 4 | 12.931 | 1.783x | 574 | 1.90x | 54272 |
| `glrd_50` (G-LRD gs 4) | 64-288 (mean 107) shared | 192-512 (mean 404) shared | **10.716** | **1.477x** | **543** | **2.00x** | **57344** |
| `palu_full_70` (M-LRD) | 32-128 (mean 58) x 4 | 96-128 (mean 121) x 4 | 9.028 | 1.245x | 761 | 1.43x | 40960 |
| `glrd_70` (G-LRD gs 4) | 160-512 (mean 228) shared | 352-512 (mean 486) shared | **8.216** | **1.133x** | 759 | 1.43x | 40960 |

- **Ratio 0.5: 17.1 % lower perplexity (10.716 vs 12.931) for 5.4 % fewer KV bytes** (543 vs 574 B
  per token per layer per chip). Measured as excess over stock, G-LRD removes **39 % of M-LRD's
  perplexity penalty** (0.477 vs 0.783 above 1.00x). The paper's Llama-3-8B-Instruct numbers move the
  same way and further: 10.11 vs 12.38 against a 8.28 baseline, i.e. 1.22x vs 1.50x, so G-LRD there
  removes 55 % of the penalty. Ours is the same effect at about two thirds the size — see §6 for why
  our M-LRD row starts from a worse place than the paper's.
- **Ratio 0.7: 9.0 % lower perplexity (8.216 vs 9.028) at the same bytes** (759 vs 761 — a 0.3 %
  difference, inside rounding of the per-layer rank means). `glrd_70` is within **13.3 %** of the
  stock model's perplexity, the closest any compressed row in this study gets.
- **Why the rank shapes look so different.** G-LRD's group latents are much wider than M-LRD's
  per-head latents (mean r_k 107 and r_v 404 at ratio 0.5, against 33 and 102), but each one is
  shared by all four KV heads on the chip, so the per-token cost is `r_k_g + r_v_g = 511` elements
  against M-LRD's `4 x (33 + 102) = 540`. The K/V split the rank search chooses is nearly identical
  in the two recipes: K is kept at 21 % of full rank under G-LRD (107/512) and 26 % under M-LRD
  (33/128), V at 79 % (404/512) and 80 % (102/128). That is spec §3's warning made visible: **at ratio 0.5 the G-LRD V latent is 404/512 wide, i.e. V is barely
  compressed at all**, and essentially all of the compression comes from K. That is also what makes
  the absorbed-V trick unusable on this path (§3).
- The quality gain at equal bytes is the paper's central claim for G-LRD, and it reproduces here:
  one rank-107 subspace fitted jointly across the four heads' 512-dimensional concatenated K space
  captures more than four independently fitted rank-33 subspaces of 128 dimensions each, because
  neighbouring KV heads are correlated.

## 3. Decode latency: the fused kernel is essential for G-LRD, and G-LRD is slower than M-LRD

Whole decode step, batch 32, `min_ms`:

| row | 1024 | 4096 | 8192 |
|---|---|---|---|

| `palu_full_70_fk2` | 43.1 (0.88x) | 66.4 (0.83x) | 97.7 |
| `glrd_50` naive | 189.2 (0.20x) | 594.7 (0.09x) | 1113.1 |
| `glrd_50_fk2` | 54.1 (0.70x) | 105.5 (0.53x) | 174.0 |

| `glrd_70_fk2` | 59.6 (0.64x) | 121.3 (0.46x) | 203.4 |

- **The fused kernel buys 3.5-6.4x on the G-LRD rows** (189.2 -> 54.1 at 1k, 594.7 -> 105.5 at 4k,
  1113.1 -> 174.0 at 8k for ratio 0.5; 3.66x and 5.62x at 1k/4k for ratio 0.7). That is a larger
  factor than it bought on the M-LRD rows in stage 2 (2.1x / 4.1x / 5.2x) because the naive G-LRD
  path reconstructs **both** K and V from group latents, expanding each latent over four heads
  before a full-width `[32, 4, S, 128]` materialisation on each side.
- **Naive G-LRD at ratio 0.7 does not fit at 8k, batch 32** (`bench_decode_glrd_70` records a
  `TT_FATAL ... Out of Memory` for the 8192 cell) — the naive path's transient reconstruction
  buffers, not the caches, are what fail; the fused row at the same ratio runs there in 203.4 ms.
  The error cell is kept in the JSON and in the table above as data.
- **G-LRD costs 1.26-2.08x M-LRD's fused decode step** (54.1/105.5/174.0 against 42.8/65.0/94.5 at
  ratio 0.5; 59.6/121.3/203.4 against 43.1/66.4/97.7 at ratio 0.7) and is **0.46-0.70x stock**, so
  the stage-2 near-parity with stock is lost. This was predicted in spec §4 ("G-LRD decode will be
  slower than M-LRD and probably slower than stock, while remaining far ahead of the naive path") and
  it is exactly what happened. The memory and accuracy columns are what G-LRD buys with it.

| `glrd_70_fk2` | 4 reads x 160 | 4 reads x 512 | 2688 | 2.63x | 2.48 / 3.58 / 6.42 | **0.563** |
| `glrd_50` naive | full-width rebuild | full-width rebuild | - | - | 5.80 / 19.25 / 36.20 | **4.343** |
| `glrd_70` naive | full-width rebuild | full-width rebuild | - | - | 6.30 / 20.89 / 39.13 | **4.690** |

- **The direction and the ordering the spec predicted hold; the magnitude does not.** Each of the
  four head work-items rebuilds its head from the whole group latent, so G-LRD reads each latent four
  times — 2304 elements per position at ratio 0.5 against M-LRD's 640 and stock's 1024, a **3.6x**
  traffic ratio to M-LRD. The measured slope ratio is **2.25x** (0.583 vs 0.259), and at 8k the
  attention block costs **1.7x** M-LRD's (5.46 vs 3.16 ms). The fourfold read is real and is the
  dominant term, but it does not cost its full nominal factor.
- **The reconstruction *arithmetic* is nowhere near proportional to the cost, which confirms stage
  2's conclusion that this kernel is overhead-bound rather than bandwidth- or FLOP-bound.** Per
  position per chip, layer 0, G-LRD 0.5 does `4 x 64 x 128` MACs to rebuild K plus `4 x 512 x 128` to
  rebuild V = 294,912, against M-LRD's 16,384 (K only; V is absorbed into `wo`) — an **18x**
  arithmetic ratio that produces a 2.25x slope ratio. In-kernel V reconstruction, the new mode this
  stage added, is 89 % of that arithmetic and is close to free: `glrd_70_fk2` has 2.5x `glrd_50_fk2`'s
  K rank and the *same* V rank, and its slope is **lower** (0.563 vs 0.583), i.e. inside the noise of
  this bench. What the fused path pays for is per-chunk overhead and the flash-decode machinery, as
  in stage 2, plus the latent reads.
- **Unlike stage 2, the two G-LRD attention rows measure genuinely different work.** In stage 2 both
  M-LRD dumps had identical layer-0 ranks (32/128), so their attention columns were the same
  measurement twice; here layer 0 is 64/512 against 160/512, which is why `glrd_70_fk2` starts higher
  at 1k (2.48 vs 1.38 ms) yet converges by 8k. The layer-0 caveat cuts the other way for the whole-step
  numbers: layer 0 is the *narrowest* K layer of `glrd_0.5` (64 against a 107 mean) and the *widest*
  V layer (512 against a 404 mean), so `32 x attention-only` is not a good model of the step here.

## 4. Decode fidelity: the fused path is faithful, including the new in-kernel V

`decode_check`, one shared prefill step plus 63 teacher-forced decode steps, naive against fused:

| row | PCC min / mean | argmax agree | row ppl |
|---|---|---|---|
| `glrd_50_fk2` | 0.9976 / 0.9991 | 0.905 | 10.716 |
| `glrd_70_fk2` | 0.9978 / 0.9989 | **0.984** | 8.216 |
| `palu_full_50_fk2` (stage 2) | 0.9980 / 0.9994 | 0.921 | 12.931 |
| `palu_full_70_fk2` (stage 2) | 0.9988 / 0.9993 | 0.984 | 9.028 |

**Both G-LRD rows clear the PCC bar (>= 0.99) with the same margin the M-LRD rows did**, and this is
a strictly stronger check than stage 2's: the G-LRD fused kernel rebuilds *V* on core as well as K,
so the V path is inside the comparison for the first time (stage 2's fused rows took V from the
absorbed-`wo` path on both sides of the comparison). Adding V reconstruction to the kernel costs
about 4e-4 of logit PCC against the naive path (0.9976 vs 0.9980 minimum at ratio 0.5) — nothing.

The argmax column behaves exactly as stage 2 described it: it tracks **how decisive the model is**,
not how accurate the op is. `glrd_50_fk2` has a marginally *lower* PCC than `palu_full_50_fk2` and a
lower agreement (0.905 vs 0.921), but `glrd_70_fk2` and `palu_full_70_fk2` — the two least damaged
rows — both sit at 0.984. Ordering the four rows by agreement (0.984, 0.984, 0.921, 0.905) reproduces
the ordering by perplexity (8.216, 9.028, 12.931, 10.716) far better than the ordering by PCC. Read
the PCC column for the kernel.

| row | max context @ batch 8 | first failing context | x stock | KV reduction (§2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | 28672 | 29696 | 1.00x | 1.00x |
| `palu_full_70_fk2` | 40960 | 41984 | 1.43x | 1.43x |
| `glrd_70_fk2` | 40960 | 41984 | 1.43x | 1.43x |

Every failure is a genuine `TT_FATAL ... Out of Memory` allocation error, bisected to 1k

## 6. Caveats

- **32 Fisher samples, not the paper's 2048.** Palu's rank search hard-codes 2048 calibration
  samples; stage 1 patched it to the command-line default of 32 because 2048 in fp32 on this CPU was
  estimated at more than a day. Both G-LRD dumps and both M-LRD dumps were calibrated this way. This
  is the single largest known difference from the paper and the most plausible cause of our larger
  compression penalties (§2, §1); the 256-sample sensitivity run that spec §5 offered as an optional
  check was not done (§7), so the size of this effect is still unmeasured rather than bounded.
- **Different model revision.** The paper evaluates Llama-3-8B-Instruct; every number here is
  Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. Our stock model is a different (and on wikitext-2 a stronger) starting point
  — 7.254 against the paper's 8.28 — so absolute rows are not comparable and only gaps are.
- **Perplexity is measured over 2048-token windows** (141 of them over wikitext-2 test); the paper
  uses 4096. Shorter windows raise perplexity for everyone, which is part of why our baseline reads
  below theirs rather than above.
- **Device numerics.** Weights and latent caches are bfp8, activations bf16, TP=2 across the two
  n300 chips, and the whole evaluation runs on device; the paper's numbers are GPU fp16. Stage 1
  measured the device-vs-CPU-fp32 perplexity difference at about 1.4 % for the stock row (7.254 vs
  7.357) and 1.3 % for `palu_full_50` (12.931 vs 12.759), so this is a small term next to the
  calibration difference, but it is not zero and it was not re-measured for the G-LRD rows (§7).
- **The metric definition differs from the paper's on two of the five tasks.** The paper reports
  `acc_norm` for OpenBookQA, HellaSwag and ARC-Challenge and `acc` for PIQA, ARC-Easy and WinoGrande;
  the rendered `avg (5)` column above is plain `acc` on all five. Both numbers are in every accuracy
  cell (`acc (acc_norm)`), and §1 quotes the paper-protocol average alongside the rendered one.
- **The fused kernel's V mode is not free of consequences even though it is nearly free of time.**
  Not absorbing V into `wo` (spec §3) means the G-LRD decode path keeps the stock output projection
  and rebuilds V per head on core; §3 shows that costs almost nothing in slope, but it does mean the
  G-LRD rows carry a V latent of up to 512 through the cache, which is why ratio 0.5 compresses K
  hard and V barely at all, and why `glrd_70`'s KV bytes are indistinguishable from `palu_full_70`'s.
- **Task accuracy runs prefill only** and therefore never enters the fused decode kernel: the five
  accuracy rows are naive-path measurements, and decode-path fidelity is `decode_check`'s job (§4).
- **Untraced decode.** Every latency number here is untraced and partly host-dispatch-bound, as in
  stages 1 and 2; `p50` and `min` agree to within 1 % on every fused row.

## 7. What was not measured

- **HellaSwag** — the paper's sixth task and about two thirds of its total scored sequences. Dropped
  on the user's time budget before the stage started, which is why `avg (5)` and not `avg (6)` is the
  comparable column everywhere above.
- **J-LRD** (one joint latent for all KV heads of a layer). The paper's best variant; deferred at
  spec time, so this study measures the two ends of the group-size axis (1 and 4) and not the third.
- **The 256-sample Fisher sensitivity calibration** (`glrd_0.5_f256` in spec §5) was optional and was
  not run, so the 32-vs-2048 calibration gap remains an explanation rather than a measurement.
- **Batch-32 max context for the G-LRD rows** (`bench_maxctx_glrd_*`): the capacity probe was run at
  batch 8 only, per the brief, so the `max ctx @ batch 32` cells for `glrd_50`/`glrd_70` in the
  quality table are dashes. Both rows do allocate and run at 8192 at batch 32 in the decode bench,
  which is where stock fails, but the ceiling above that was not bisected.
- **Batch sweeps and context scaling for the G-LRD rows** (stage 2's §5/§6 matrices), **prefill/TTFT
  for the G-LRD rows**, **CPU fp32 perplexity for the G-LRD rows**, and **fused Phase 1 for G-LRD**
  (Phase 2 only was measured; Phase 1 is a correctness checkpoint that stage 2 already retired).
- **Generation samples** for any G-LRD row.

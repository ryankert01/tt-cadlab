Two independent results, reported side by side because stage 2b left both as its top follow-ups.

## 1. Calibration: 256 Fisher samples move the gap, and by more than the noise

Stage 2b's leading explanation for our G-LRD shortfall against the paper was the 32-sample Fisher
calibration, and it was untested. It is now tested at one point on the ladder, 32 -> 256, and the
answer is **yes, it is a real cause, and it is not the whole cause.**

Paper-protocol five-task average (`acc_norm` for OpenBookQA and ARC-Challenge, `acc` for the rest),
and the gap to our own stock row (67.265):

| row | avg (5) paper protocol | gap to stock | plain `acc` avg (5) | gap, plain `acc` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | 67.265 | — | 65.437 | — |
| `glrd_50` (32 Fisher samples) | 62.177 | **-5.088** | 59.483 | -5.954 |
| `glrd_50_f256` (256 samples) | **63.201** | **-4.064** | 60.781 | -4.657 |
| `glrd_70` (32 samples) | 66.120 | **-1.145** | 64.094 | -1.344 |
| `glrd_70_f256` (256 samples) | **67.032** | **-0.233** | 64.533 | -0.905 |

**Both ratios improve, by +1.024 and +0.913 points of paper-protocol average.** The spec set the bar
at "more than the bootstrap noise, ~0.5 points on 22k scored sequences" and said movement in either
direction is a result. Both clear it on the paper protocol. Two honesty notes on that claim:

- On the **plain-`acc`** column the ratio-0.5 movement is larger (+1.298) but the ratio-0.7 movement is
  **+0.439, i.e. at or just below the noise bar**. So the ratio-0.5 result is unambiguous on either
  metric, and the ratio-0.7 result is above the bar on the paper's metric definition and marginal on
  the other. Do not read the 0.7 row as a second independent confirmation of the same size.
- What does make the 0.7 row more than a coin flip is that **all five tasks move the same way**
  (+0.38 to +1.40), a coherent shift rather than one task carrying the average. Five independent
  positive moves is itself unlikely under noise, even where each is individually small.

`glrd_70_f256` lands **0.233 points below the stock model** on the paper protocol at a 1.43x KV
reduction. That is the closest any compressed row in this study gets to stock, and close enough that
the remaining difference is inside the measurement noise.

### 1.1 Against the paper: 53 % of the shortfall closed, and it is the wrong 53 %

The paper's G-LRD at 50 % compression loses 3.17 points of five-task average against its own baseline;
stage 2b's `glrd_50` lost 5.09, a **1.92-point shortfall** that stage 2b could not explain. Per task,
gap to the row's own baseline:

| task | paper G-LRD | ours, 32 samples | ours, 256 samples | f256 - f32 | f256 - paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OpenBookQA | -0.60 | -2.80 | -3.20 | **-0.40** | -2.60 |
| PIQA | -2.56 | -4.03 | -3.26 | +0.76 | -0.70 |
| ARC-Easy | -5.31 | -7.66 | -4.08 | **+3.58** | **+1.23** |
| ARC-Challenge | -7.84 | -7.17 | -6.14 | +1.02 | **+1.70** |
| WinoGrande | **+0.48** | -3.79 | -3.63 | **+0.16** | **-4.11** |
| **avg (5)** | **-3.17** | **-5.09** | **-4.06** | **+1.02** | **-0.90** |

The shortfall against the paper goes from **-1.92 to -0.90: 53 % of it is calibration.** But the
per-task breakdown says the recovered half and the stubborn half are different tasks:

- **ARC-Easy alone supplies +3.58 of the +5.12 points of total per-task movement**, and it overshoots:
  at 256 samples we are 1.23 points *better* than the paper on ARC-Easy. ARC-Challenge adds +1.02 and
  also ends up ahead of the paper (+1.70).
- **WinoGrande, which was stage 2b's single largest divergence from the paper, barely moved: +0.16.**
  It remains **-4.11** against the paper's +0.48, and after the recalibration it is essentially the
  *entire* remaining shortfall: WinoGrande contributes -4.11/5 = -0.82 of the -0.90 average, while the
  other four tasks together now average -0.09 (-2.60, -0.70, +1.23, +1.70), i.e. they collectively
  match the paper. Stage 2b named WinoGrande as the biggest cell difference and offered calibration as the
  leading explanation; **this measurement refutes that for WinoGrande specifically** and leaves the
  model-revision hypothesis (Llama-3.1 vs Llama-3, stage 2b §1 point 2) as the standing candidate.
- OpenBookQA got **slightly worse** (-0.40). On 500 documents that is two questions; it is noise, but
  it is not movement in the hypothesised direction either.

So the honest summary is: **calibration explains about half the G-LRD shortfall, and the half it
explains is not the half stage 2b pointed at.**

### 1.2 Perplexity moves much less than task accuracy

| row | ppl (device, 141 windows) | x stock | share of M-LRD's ppl penalty removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | 7.254 | 1.000 | — |
| `glrd_50` | 10.716 | 1.477 | 39.0 % |
| `glrd_50_f256` | **10.561** | **1.456** | **41.7 %** |
| `glrd_70` | 8.216 | 1.133 | 45.8 % |
| `glrd_70_f256` | **8.135** | **1.122** | **50.3 %** |

Perplexity improves by **1.44 %** at ratio 0.5 and **0.98 %** at ratio 0.7 — real, consistent in sign
with the task result, and much smaller in relative terms. Measured as the share of M-LRD's penalty
that G-LRD removes, perplexity moves 39.0 -> 41.7 % while the task-based recovery share moves
**39.3 -> 51.5 %** (the paper's is 64.6 %). Stage 2b made a point of the two measures agreeing to
within a percent; **at 256 samples they no longer agree**, and the task metric is the one that moved.

The most likely reading is that better Fisher statistics mostly reallocate rank between layers rather
than reduce reconstruction error overall, and that multiple-choice scoring is more sensitive to that
reallocation than a token-averaged likelihood is. §1.3 shows the reallocation is small enough to make
this plausible.

### 1.3 Why the movement is small: 8x the samples changes almost nothing about the ranks

The 256-sample Fisher pass costs 4.7 h of CPU and changes the rank allocation remarkably little:

| dump | r_k range (mean) | r_v range (mean) | r_k + r_v mean | layers with identical (r_k, r_v) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `glrd_0.5` | 64-288 (107) | 192-512 (404) | 511 | — |
| `glrd_0.5_f256` | 64-288 (**113**) | 192-512 (**400**) | 513 | **22 / 32** |
| `glrd_0.7` | 160-512 (228) | 352-512 (486) | 714 | — |
| `glrd_0.7_f256` | 160-480 (**234**) | 352-512 (**483**) | 717 | **22 / 32** |

**22 of 32 layers are bit-identical in their rank choice at both ratios**, and of the 64 rank entries
per dump only 10 (ratio 0.5) and 11 (ratio 0.7) changed — every one of them by exactly one 32-wide
step, the search's granularity. The direction is consistent and interpretable: **K gains, V gives
back, in the early layers.** At ratio 0.5, layers 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 and 9 each gain 32 of K rank while
layers 13, 14, 30 and 31 each give up 32 of V. At ratio 0.7 the same pattern holds (seven K gains in
layers 2-20, three V losses, one K loss at layer 31). The total budget is unchanged by construction
(511 -> 513 and 714 -> 717, inside the rounding of a per-layer mean).

That is the mechanism behind the whole result. Eight times the Fisher samples does not find a
different decomposition; it moves about a sixth of the rank budget one notch, from the barely
compressed V side toward the heavily compressed K side of the early layers, and that is worth about
one point of task accuracy and one percent of perplexity. It also explains why the run-time cost is
unchanged: `glrd_50_f256_fk2` decodes at 54.19 / 105.82 / 174.76 ms against `glrd_50_fk2`'s
54.07 / 105.49 / 174.02 at batch 32, a difference of 0.2-0.4 %, because the rank *sums* the kernel
actually reads are the same.

### 1.4 Verdict on the paper-gap hypothesis

Stage 2b's hypothesis was that the 32-sample Fisher calibration is the leading cause of our G-LRD
shortfall, and that it should hurt G-LRD more than M-LRD because a group's whitening and rank search
estimate far more parameters from the same data. **Partly confirmed.** Going to 256 samples recovers
53 % of the shortfall and lifts G-LRD's recovery of M-LRD's loss from 39 % to 52 % against the paper's
65 %. The remaining -0.90 is now concentrated in a single task, WinoGrande, which did not respond to
calibration at all and whose -4.11 against the paper is the largest single unexplained cell in this
study.

Two things this experiment does **not** settle. It moves the Fisher pass only — Palu's whitening
already used its own default of 256 samples at every setting, so the whitening limb of stage 2b's
argument is untested and the whitening transform is identical between a dump and its `_f256`
counterpart. And the paper uses **2048** samples, 8x more again; §1.3's evidence that the ranks are
converging (22/32 layers already stable, every change one notch) suggests the remaining 8x buys less
than this one did, but that is an inference from one step, not a measurement of the endpoint.

## 2. The shared-latent kernel: the read sharing is real and small, and `auto` gives it away

The premise (spec §3): each of the four head work-items of a G-LRD group re-reads the same group
latent chunk, so a core that owns several heads of one group should read the chunk once and rebuild
all of its heads from it. That is what the kernel now does. It does not produce the speed-up the spec
projected, and the measurements say fairly precisely why.

### 2.1 The three work layouts, and which of them pays for a reduction

Everything below follows from this table. On the n300 at batch 32 (64 cores, 4 KV heads per chip, one
group of 4):

| layout | items | cores per item | **reduction rounds** | latent reads per chunk, per core |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| per-head (stage 2b) | 32 x 4 = 128 | 1 (2 heads per core) | **0** | 2 |
| group H = 2 | 32 x 2 = 64 | 1 | **0** | 1 |
| group H = 4 | 32 x 1 = 32 | 2 | **1** | 1 |

**H = 2 at batch 32 is an exact A/B against the per-head kernel**: the same 64 cores, the same two
heads per core, no cross-core reduction on either side, and exactly half the latent reads. It is the
clean measurement of the spec's hypothesis, and it was added to this stage after the first `auto`
numbers came back slower than stage 2b. H = 4 changes two things at once — it halves the reads again
*and* it introduces a reduction round that the per-head kernel at this batch does not pay, settled
through the slow primitive correction sequence of §6.1.

At batch 8 the comparison is different and worse: the per-head kernel is 32 items over 2 cores each
(one round) while group H = 2 is 16 items over 4 each (**two** rounds), and H = 4 is rejected by the
occupancy rule because the 4-cores-per-item cap of §6.3 would leave half the grid idle.

### 2.2 Whole decode step, batch 32: -1.6 % at H = 2, +0.7 % under `auto`

`min_ms`, and the per-layer H each run actually used (`group_heads_per_layer` in the JSON):

| row | per-layer H | 1k | 4k | 8k |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `glrd_50_fk2` (per-head) | 1 x 32 | 54.1 | 105.5 | 174.0 |
| `glrd_50_fk2g2` (H = 2 forced) | **2 x 32** | **53.8 (-0.5 %)** | **103.8 (-1.6 %)** | **170.4 (-2.1 %)** |
| `glrd_50_fk2g` (`auto`) | **4 x 15, 2 x 17** | 55.5 (+2.6 %) | 106.2 (+0.7 %) | 173.6 (-0.2 %) |
| `glrd_70_fk2` (per-head) | 1 x 32 | 59.6 | 121.2 | 203.4 |
| `glrd_70_fk2g2` (H = 2 forced) | **2 x 31, 1 x 1** | **59.5 (-0.2 %)** | **119.8 (-1.2 %)** | **200.2 (-1.6 %)** |
| `glrd_70_fk2g` (`auto`) | 4 x 1, 2 x 30, 1 x 1 | 59.6 (+0.0 %) | 119.9 (-1.1 %) | 200.4 (-1.5 %) |

Three things, in order of how much they matter:

1. **Halving the latent reads is worth about 1.5-2 % of the whole decode step, growing with context.**
   Both dumps agree: -1.6 % / -2.1 % at 4k / 8k for ratio 0.5, -1.2 % / -1.6 % for ratio 0.7. It is a
   real, reproducible, same-signed win on two dumps at three contexts. It is also roughly a
   twentieth of what spec §1 asked for.
2. **The H = 4 layout costs more than the extra read-halving buys.** On `glrd_0.5`, where `auto`
   picks H = 4 on 15 of 32 layers, the run is 1.9-3.1 percentage points worse than forced H = 2 at
   every context and is *slower than the stage 2b per-head kernel* at 1k and 4k. On `glrd_0.7`, where
   `auto` picks H = 4 on exactly one layer, `auto` and forced H = 2 are indistinguishable (119.9 vs
   119.8). The two dumps differ in nothing else relevant, and they differ in the H distribution by
   construction, so this is as close to a controlled attribution as this study gets: **the cost is the
   reduction round, not H itself.**
3. **`auto`'s selector is therefore the wrong rule at batch 32.** It is occupancy-first (FW.3): it
   takes the largest H that fills the grid, and at batch 32 both H = 2 and H = 4 fill it, so ties go to
   the larger H. But H = 4 fills the grid only by splitting each item over two cores, which is exactly
   the cost. **Follow-up for the selector (not changed here — this stage does not modify the kernel or
   its host): prefer the largest H whose layout needs no cross-core reduction, i.e. cap H so that
   `cores_per_item == 1`, and only then break ties by occupancy.** On this hardware that yields H = 2
   at batch 32 and H = 1 at batch 8, which is precisely the configuration that measures best in every
   cell of this table and of §2.4.

### 2.3 Attention-only, and a warning about it

Layer 0 only (the bench builds one layer), batch 32:

| row | H at layer 0 | 1k | 4k | 8k | slope (ms/1k) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `glrd_50_fk2` | per-head | 1.382 | 3.102 | 5.461 | **0.583** |
| `glrd_50_fk2g2` | 2 | 1.711 | 3.470 | 5.323 | **0.516** |
| `glrd_50_fk2g` | 4 | 1.547 | 3.766 | 6.034 | **0.641** |
| `glrd_70_fk2` | per-head | 2.479 | 3.576 | 6.424 | **0.564** |
| `glrd_70_fk2g` (`auto`) | 2 | 1.519 | 3.404 | 5.829 | **0.616** |
| `glrd_70_fk2g2` (forced) | 2 | 1.490 | 3.760 | 5.872 | **0.626** |

**Read the last two rows first: they are the same configuration measured twice.** `auto` chose H = 2
at `glrd_0.7`'s layer 0, and the forced run asked for H = 2, so the two runs differ only in when they
ran. They agree to 2 % at 1k and 0.7 % at 8k and **disagree by 10.5 % at 4k** (3.404 vs 3.760). That
is an accidental repeatability check, and it is the most useful single number in this section:
**the attention-only 4k cell carries about 10 % of run-to-run noise, which is larger than most of the
group-vs-per-head differences in this table.** Every slope in the rendered table above inherits it.
So:

- The slope column should not be used to rank these rows. `glrd_70_fk2g`'s slope (0.616) is *worse*
  than the per-head row's (0.564) while its absolute time is lower at all three contexts — the slope
  fell out of a much lower 1k intercept, not out of a steeper cost per token. Slope is a two-point
  statistic on noisy points here.
- The one clean signal is `glrd_50`'s H = 2 row: slope 0.583 -> **0.516**, an 11.5 % reduction in
  marginal cost per 1k of context, with a *higher* intercept (1.382 -> 1.711). That is exactly the
  shape read-sharing should produce — less work per chunk, plus a fixed per-call cost for the
  restructured loop — and it is why the win in §2.2 grows with context.
- `glrd_70_fk2`'s 1k cell (2.479) was already flagged as anomalous in stage 2b. Two independent
  group runs put the same layer at 1.490 and 1.519, so the ~1 ms gap there is reproducible on the
  group side; whether the per-head side is really that slow at 1k was not re-measured.

### 2.4 Batch 8: a uniform regression, and the reduction count explains it

| row | per-layer H | 1k | 4k | 8k |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `glrd_50_fk2` (per-head, 1 round) | 1 x 32 | 39.5 | 54.1 | 73.6 |
| `glrd_50_fk2g` (2 rounds) | 2 x 32 | 42.1 (**+6.7 %**) | 57.1 (**+5.6 %**) | 77.1 (**+4.7 %**) |
| `glrd_70_fk2` (per-head, 1 round) | 1 x 32 | 41.3 | 58.5 | 81.3 |
| `glrd_70_fk2g` (2 rounds) | 2 x 31, 1 x 1 | 44.7 (**+8.1 %**) | 62.2 (**+6.3 %**) | 85.7 (**+5.3 %**) |

Group mode is **4.7-8.1 % slower at batch 8 on both dumps**, and the penalty shrinks monotonically
with context — read-sharing eating into a fixed cost it never overcomes. This is the one place the
stage produced an outright regression against stage 2b's kernel, and it is the layout where the group
path pays *two* reduction rounds against the per-head path's one. It is also the layout the §6.3 cap
forces: without the 4-cores-per-item cap, H = 4 at batch 8 would be 8 items over 8 cores, which
deadlocks.

Note that `auto` picked these H values, so **the shipped default is slower than stage 2b at batch 8**.
Under the §2.2 follow-up rule (no reduction beyond what the per-head kernel already pays) `auto` would
return H = 1 here and the regression would disappear.

### 2.5 Where the remaining time actually goes

The G-LRD decode step is attention-dominated, and the gap to M-LRD is almost entirely attention. At
4k, batch 32: `glrd_50_fk2` costs 105.5 ms against `palu_full_50_fk2`'s 65.0 ms, a difference of
**40.5 ms**; 32 layers of the layer-0 attention-only difference (3.102 - 1.710) predicts **44.5 ms**.
Those agree to within the noise of §2.3, so the step difference is attention and nothing else, and any
route to the spec's 80 ms runs through the attention block.

That also sets the scale of what read-sharing could ever have delivered here. Sharing the read removes
at most the duplicate *traffic*, and the measured value of removing half of it is 1.5-2 % of the step.
To reach 80 ms from 105.5 the step has to lose 25.5 ms — about a quarter — and by the paragraph above
essentially all of it would have to come out of attention. Stage 2b already concluded this kernel is overhead-bound rather than bandwidth-bound — an 18x
arithmetic ratio over M-LRD producing a 2.25x slope ratio — and stage 2c is the direct test of that
conclusion: **we removed up to three quarters of the duplicated latent traffic and got 2 % of the
step.** The remaining cost is per-chunk flash-decode machinery and the reconstruction arithmetic
itself, neither of which the group layout touches.

## 3. Decode fidelity: the group kernel is faithful; its problem is speed, not correctness

`decode_check`, one shared prefill step plus 63 teacher-forced decode steps at batch 1, naive against
fused. The `_fk2g` rows ran with `--group-heads 2`, which is that harness's default and not `auto`:
at batch 1 `auto` returns H = 1 for every layer (§6.3), so `auto` there would have measured the stage
2b per-head kernel and told us nothing. H = 2 exercises the group branch **and** its sequence-split
reduction — at batch 1 an item is split over the capped 4 cores, i.e. two reduction rounds — on every
layer, which is the strongest fidelity test available.

| row | per-layer H | PCC min / mean | argmax agree |
|---|---|---|---|
| `glrd_50_fk2` (stage 2b) | per-head | 0.99761 / 0.99910 | 0.905 |
| `glrd_50_fk2g` | **2 x 32** | **0.99473 / 0.99908** | 0.952 |
| `glrd_70_fk2` (stage 2b) | per-head | 0.99783 / 0.99891 | 0.984 |
| `glrd_70_fk2g` | **2 x 31, 1 x 1** | **0.99718 / 0.99892** | 0.984 |
| `glrd_50_f256_fk2` | 1 x 32 | 0.99750 / 0.99908 | 0.937 |
| `glrd_70_f256_fk2` | 1 x 32 | 0.99774 / 0.99892 | 0.937 |

**Mean PCC is preserved to five decimal places on both group rows** (0.99908 vs 0.99910, 0.99892 vs
0.99891). Only the minimum moves, and only on ratio 0.5: 0.99761 -> 0.99473, a drop of 0.0029 on the
worst of 63 steps. That is the expected and predicted signature of §6.2's six extra bf16 packs per
child merge, accumulated over two reduction rounds, and it is comfortably inside the >= 0.99 bar every
stage of this study has used. The argmax column moves in the *opposite* direction (0.905 -> 0.952),
which is the behaviour stage 2b documented: argmax agreement tracks how decisive the model is at each
step, not how accurate the op is. Read the PCC column for the kernel.

The op-level bit-identity evidence is separate and is described in §6.2: `torch.equal` where the work
split is unchanged, PCC 0.9999945-0.9999992 group-vs-per-head where it is not.

## 5. Verdict against the spec's success criteria

Spec §1 set five bars. Two are met, two are missed, one is met with a recorded and understood
deviation.

| # | criterion | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | calibration: five-task gap moves by more than the ~0.5-point bootstrap noise | -5.088 -> **-4.064** (ratio 0.5), -1.145 -> **-0.233** (ratio 0.7), paper protocol | **met** |
| 2a | group output bit-identical to per-head for the same inputs | `torch.equal` where the split is unchanged; PCC 0.9999945+ where it is not | **met with deviation** |
| 2b | attention slope at batch 32 for `glrd_50` from 0.583 toward <= 0.35 | best measured **0.516** (H = 2); `auto` gives 0.641 | **missed** |
| 2c | decode step at 4k, batch 32, from 105.5 to <= 80 ms | best measured **103.8 ms** (H = 2); `auto` gives 106.2 | **missed** |
| 2d | no regression of the per-head path | unchanged by construction and by op test; **but see below** | **partially evidenced** |

**On 2b.** 0.583 -> 0.516 is an 11.5 % improvement in marginal cost and it is in the right direction,
but the target was a 40 % cut to reach M-LRD's neighbourhood and it is not close. Worse, the shipped
`auto` configuration makes the slope *worse* (0.641). And §2.3 shows the metric itself carries ~10 %
noise at its 4k point, so even the 0.516 should be read as "roughly 0.52 +/- a lot".

**On 2c.** 105.5 -> 103.8 ms is 1.6 %, against a target of 24 %. The measurements say why with
unusual clarity (§2.5): the whole duplicated-latent-read term, which the spec identified as the
problem and which this stage successfully removed up to three quarters of, is worth about 2 % of the
step. The premise that the fourfold latent read was the dominant cost was **wrong**; stage 2b's own
conclusion that the kernel is overhead-bound rather than bandwidth-bound was right, and this stage is
the controlled experiment that settles it.

**On 2d, honestly.** The per-head path is the `#else` branch of the same kernel, is bit-identical to
stage 2b by `test_heads_per_core_1_is_the_stock_split`, and all 53 op tests and 23 attention device
tests pass. But the batch-32 `_fk2` rows in the table above are the **stage 2b JSONs, not re-runs**,
so no fresh timing confirms the absence of a performance regression at that batch; the only new
per-head timings are the batch-8 rows, which have no stage 2b counterpart. The criterion is met
structurally and by test, not by a repeated measurement, and it would have been cheap to re-measure.

**A sixth outcome the spec did not ask for, and the most actionable one:** the shipped `auto` selector
is a net *regression* against stage 2b at batch 8 (+4.7 to +8.1 %) and at batch 32 on `glrd_0.5` at 1k
and 4k (+2.6 %, +0.7 %). Under the §2.2 rule — never choose an H that adds a cross-core reduction the
per-head kernel would not pay — every one of those cells becomes a small win instead. That change is
in the host's `group_heads_per_core` selector, not in the kernel, and it was deliberately **not** made
in this stage (Task 4 does not modify Python or kernel sources); it is the first thing stage 3 should
do, and it should be re-measured, not assumed.

## 6. Four kernel findings that are results in their own right

### 6.1 tt-metal's `correction_block` overflows the DST window under fp32 accumulation

This one is an upstream bug report, not a Palu result. `correction_block` is tt-metal's fused
flash-decode helper for merging one child's `(m, l, O)` into a parent's: six steps — new max, two
`exp` corrections, two rescales, one add — kept in DST across a single `tile_regs_acquire`, staging
five operands through `dst_reg_0..4`. A program compiled with `fp32_dest_acc_en` and without
`dst_full_sync_en` has a **four-tile** DST window (`dst_size = fp32_dest_acc_en ? 4 : 8`), and Phase 2
of the Palu op forces fp32 destination accumulation because the rotated-query recurrence needs it. The
fifth operand aliases. The helper then returns the *local* maximum as the merged maximum, which drives
the child's exponential correction factor to zero, and the merged output silently collapses onto the
reducer core's own slice — every batch row whose sequence was split came out as that core's partial,
normalised by that core's `l`.

It presents as a correctness bug with no error and no warning, and it was narrowed the hard way: rows
with `k_num_chunks == 1` (no child) were bit-exact while every row with a child was wrong; dropping the
child's payload entirely gave PCC 1.00000 over the root's own chunk range, so the peel, the finalize
and the writer plumbing were all correct; and reading the child's `l`, `m` and `O` back out gave
implied divisors of 32.40 / 39.84 / 33.51 against host maxima of 31.76 / 40.56 / 34.05, so the
payloads arrive intact. The failure is inside the helper.

Two experiments pin the cause to the window rather than to the phase, both on the failing layout
(batch 16, H = 2, r_k 128 / r_v 256, S 2048) with the fused helper temporarily restored, scored against
an fp32 reference:

| configuration | DST tiles | PCC |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 2, `fp32_dest_acc_en=True`, `dst_full_sync_en=False` (the failing baseline) | 4 | **0.3338** |
| Phase 2, `fp32_dest_acc_en=True`, **`dst_full_sync_en=True`** | 8 | **0.99997** |
| Phase 1, **`fp32_dest_acc_en=False`** | 8 | **0.99994** |
| Phase 1, `fp32_dest_acc_en=True` (control) | 4 | **0.3338** |

The PCC tracks the window size and nothing else. Either of the two working configurations would fix
it, and neither is available here: Phase 2 needs the fp32 destination, and `dst_full_sync_en` is a
program-wide performance change. So the group branch replaces the helper with primitives — `max_block`,
two `sub_exp_block`, `mul_block_inplace` twice, `add_block_inplace` — the same values in the same
order, from the same header, and the configuration-independent fix. The clean upstream fix is to give
`correction_block` two acquisitions.

**The per-head branch overflows the same window and gets away with it.** It still calls the helper on
every stage 2b sequence split under the same four-tile budget. Measured against the fp32 reference on
grouped latents at chunk 128, Phase 2: PCC **0.9999822** at batch 8 (one reduction round) and
**0.9993549** at batch 1 (three rounds) — ordinary bf16 merge error, nothing like 0.334. So every
stage 2b number in `docs/results/stage2b.md` stands. But it is correct by luck rather than by
construction, which is why `test_per_head_reduction_is_accurate_under_fp32_dest_acc` now pins it, and
why the README's known-issues entry says so plainly.

### 6.2 The primitive replacement is why group mode is not bit-identical when a child merges

`correction_block` keeps its six steps in fp32 DST and packs four results; the primitive sequence packs
to bf16 after every step, so a child merge costs **six extra bf16 quantisations**. That is a precision
difference, not an arithmetic one, and it is a first-class reason bit identity between the group and
per-head kernels cannot hold wherever a core has a child — independent of the fact that a different H
also changes the work split, which changes the order flash states are merged in.

So the bar is split, and both halves are met:

- **Bit-identical (`torch.equal`) where no child is merged.** `test_group_shared_batch32_matches_stock_split`
  (batch 32, stock split vs `heads_per_core=2`, three (r_k, r_v) shapes x Phase 1 and 2 — six cases),
  `test_group_shared_whole_group_on_one_core` (H = 4, batch 16, 4x4 grid, one core per group), and
  `test_heads_per_core_1_is_the_stock_split`. All pass `torch.equal` on the bf16 output.
- **PCC >= 0.9995 where a child merges**, as the plan's Global Constraints allow. Measured
  group-vs-per-head: **0.9999950-0.9999992** on the four sequence-split cases and
  **0.9999945-0.9999972** on the six batch-16 cases; against the fp32 reference, 0.99996-0.99998. At
  the attention level the largest absolute difference is 1.5e-05 on values of order 1e-4..1e-3.

The spec's criterion (a) asked for bit-identity outright. It is met for the layout the spec's own
argument covers (same work split, same chunk order) and provably cannot be met for the rest; the
recorded reason is the one the spec's risk section anticipated ("if a legitimate difference appears
... the bar drops"), except that the cause turned out to be the workaround for 6.1 rather than packer
rounding.

### 6.3 A three-round reduction tree deadlocks in group mode; the split is capped at 4 cores per item

Bisected after the correctness fix:

| layout | cores per item | reduction rounds | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| batch 32 / H = 4 | 2 | 1 | pass |
| batch 16 / H = 4 | 4 | 2 | pass |
| batch 8 / H = 2 | 4 | 2 | pass |
| batch 8 / H = 4, S = 512 (only 4 cores get a chunk) | 8 | 2 effective | pass |
| batch 8 / H = 4, S = 1024 or 4096 | 8 | 3 | **hang** |
| batch 4 / H = 2, S = 1024 | 8 | 3 | **hang** |

It is the three-round tree — the first layout with a node that has both two children *and* a parent —
and not H: the per-head kernel's batch-1 tests split over 16 cores (four rounds) and pass. The
protocol was traced for that topology (slots, semaphore nibbles, CB capacities, the
`cb_out_o == cb_out_worker` aliasing on an intermediate node) without finding the cycle, and the
watcher cannot be enabled on this build (`idle_erisc.elf ... overflows region`). A reviewer
independently ruled out the same candidates. **Cause unresolved.**

The mitigation is `group_max_cores_per_head = 4` in the program factory: group mode never builds a
three-round tree. It has a direct cost in this report's numbers — at batch 8 with H = 4 a group would
occupy 4 cores per item, 8 items, 32 of 64 cores, so half the grid idles. That is exactly why the
host's occupancy rule prefers H = 2 at batch 8 (16 items x 4 cores = 64), and why below batch 8
nothing clears the gate at all and `auto` returns H = 1. Reproducer for the hang: batch 4, S 1024,
r_k 128, r_v 256, `heads_per_core=2`.

### 6.4 The L1 ceiling is an empirical constant, with a runtime fallback behind it

H is chosen per layer from a model of the factory's own circular-buffer sum (`palu_l1_cb_bytes`),
which reproduces the allocator's reported figure exactly. The budget it is compared against is
measured, not derived — the model keeps L1-sharded activations at the top of the bank, so the real
ceiling is wherever the lowest of those lands. Measured on Llama-3.1-8B decode, `glrd_0.5` layer 0
(64/512) with H forced to 4:

| batch | outcome | bound |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | clash: *"L1 buffer allocated at 1413120 ... region ends at 1415456"* | ceiling = **1,413,120 B** |
| 16 | no clash, region ends at 1,415,648 | ceiling > 1,415,648 |
| 32 | no clash, region ends at 1,366,496 | ceiling > 1,366,496 |

The estimator budgets against the tightest of the three (batch 8) with a 16 KB safety margin. The
margin cannot be raised much: the decisive H = 4 case (128/416 at batch 32) needs the CB region to end
at 1,390,560, i.e. 22,560 B under the batch-8 ceiling, so anything above ~22.5 KB of margin would drop
the spec's H = 4 target outright. The residual risk is carried at run time instead: `PaluAttention`
catches a `RuntimeError` naming a circular buffer, halves `self.group_heads` and retries, down to
H = 1 (the stage 2b program, which stage 2b already proved fits). The per-layer H recorded in each
result JSON is written **after** any such fallback, so the `group_heads_per_layer` lists quoted in this
document are what actually ran, not what was picked.

## 7. Caveats

- **Every stage 2b caveat still applies**, unchanged: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct rather than the paper's
  Llama-3-8B-Instruct; 2048-token perplexity windows rather than 4096; bf16 activations, bfp8 weights
  and latent caches, TP=2 across the two n300 chips against the paper's GPU fp16; five tasks rather
  than six (no HellaSwag); untraced, partly host-dispatch-bound decode; the `_recon_len` workaround for
  the stock decode op's 32-row chunk defect. See `docs/results/stage2b.md` §6.
- **The Fisher calibration is still 8x short of the paper's.** 256 against 2048. §1 argues the curve
  has flattened, but that is an inference from one step of the ladder (32 -> 256), not a measurement of
  the endpoint. The 2048-sample run is ~37 h of CPU at the measured 66 s per sample and was never in
  budget.
- **`_f256` moves the Fisher pass only.** Palu's whitening already used its own default of 256 samples
  at every setting, so the whitening transform is byte-identical between a `_f256` dump and its 32-sample
  counterpart; only the rank *allocation* changes. If the conditioning of a 512x512 whitening estimate
  is what costs G-LRD its accuracy (stage 2b §1's hypothesis had two limbs, whitening and rank search),
  this experiment tests one limb and holds the other fixed. That is a real limitation of the design,
  and it is the strongest remaining reason not to close the question outright.
- **The batch-32 `_fk2` rows in the kernel table are the stage 2b measurements, not re-runs.** The
  per-head path is the `#else` branch of the same kernel and is bit-identical to stage 2b by
  `test_heads_per_core_1_is_the_stock_split`, but the no-regression bar is therefore carried by the op
  tests rather than by a repeated timing at batch 32. The only fresh per-head timings here are the
  batch-8 rows, which have no stage 2b counterpart to be compared against.
- **The attention-only bench builds layer 0 only**, so its H is layer 0's H, not the model's
  distribution: H = 4 for `glrd_0.5` (64/512) and H = 2 for `glrd_0.7` (160/512). The slope column is
  therefore a best case for ratio 0.5 and a middling case for ratio 0.7, and `32 x attention-only` is
  not a model of the decode step — layer 0 is the narrowest K layer and the widest V layer of the 0.5
  dump.
- **The attention-only bench carries about 10 % run-to-run noise at its 4k point.** Measured
  accidentally: `glrd_70_fk2g` and `glrd_70_fk2g2` are the same configuration (layer 0, H = 2, batch
  32) run 11 minutes apart and differ by 10.5 % at 4k, against 2 % at 1k and 0.7 % at 8k (§2.3). Every
  `slope` in the rendered kernel table is a two-point statistic over those points and inherits it, so
  slope differences smaller than roughly 0.1 ms/1k are not resolvable here. The whole-step decode
  numbers are far steadier and are what §2's conclusions rest on.
- **H is chosen from an L1 model against an empirically measured ceiling** (§6.4), with a runtime
  halving fallback behind it. A different model, batch or activation layout could move the ceiling; the
  numbers here are for Llama-3.1-8B decode on this build.
- **The three-round deadlock is worked around, not fixed** (§6.3). Any future change that raises
  `group_max_cores_per_head` above 4 will hit it again.
- **Task accuracy runs prefill only** and therefore never enters the fused decode kernel at all; the
  `--group-heads` flag cannot move a single accuracy number. Decode-path fidelity is `decode_check`'s
  job, and the calibration and kernel halves of this stage share no measurement.

## 8. What was not measured

- **The 2048-sample Fisher calibration** — the paper's actual setting (see above).
- **Whitening sample count.** Held fixed at Palu's default 256 in every dump in this study, so the
  whitening limb of stage 2b's calibration hypothesis is untested.
- **J-LRD**, still. One joint latent for all KV heads of a layer; the paper's best variant, deferred at
  stage 2b spec time and not revisited.
- **HellaSwag**, still, for the same time reason as stage 2b.
- **Group mode below batch 8.** `auto`'s occupancy gate returns H = 1 there, so nobody has measured
  whether a 4x cut in latent reads beats three quarters of an idle grid at batch 1 or 4. `decode_check`
  runs the group kernel at batch 1 with an explicit `--group-heads 2` for fidelity, but it is not a
  timed harness, so the question stays open. If it were ever answered "yes", the gate should become a
  cost model rather than a hard threshold.
- **Traced decode.** Every latency figure in this study, across all four stages, is untraced.
- **The per-head kernel re-timed at batch 32 on this build** (see the caveat above).
- **`eval_tasks` on the group kernel.** Meaningless by construction (prefill only), so it was not run.
- **The upstream `correction_block` fix.** §6.1 is a diagnosis and a workaround, not a patch to
  tt-metal, and no issue has been filed.

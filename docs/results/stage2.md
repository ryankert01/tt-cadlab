# Stage 2 results: fused K-reconstruction decode kernel on a Wormhole n300

Generated by `python -m palu_tt.report --stage 2` from `docs/results/raw/*.json` at commit `521b725`; tt-metal v0.78.0 plus the `palu-sdpa` submodule branch, TP=2 (one n300, two chips), bfp8 weights and latent caches, non-paged, untraced decode, `use_qk_fused=False` for every row.

A `_fk1`/`_fk2` row is the row above it re-run with `--fused p1` / `--fused p2`. Perplexity here is measured **through prefill windows**, which reconstruct K and V in flight and never touch the decode kernel, so a fused row's perplexity and prefill time are its base row's by construction and are not re-measured; KV bytes and max context are inherited too unless the row has its own `bench_maxctx_*` file, in which case that measurement wins. The decode path is checked instead by `palu_tt.decode_check` (naive vs fused, teacher-forced), whose two columns are in the quality table.

## 1. Success bar

Spec §1 sets three criteria. **Two are met, one is met in part.**

**(1) Numerical equivalence to the stage-1 naive path — MET.** Op level (`tests/test_palu_sdpa_device.py`,
20 tests, not the full 3×3×2×2 grid the envelope would allow): r_k ∈ {32, 64, 96} at S = 1024, both
phases, batch ∈ {1, 32}; Phase 2 additionally at S ∈ {4096, 8192} (r_k = 64, batch 1) and S = 8192
(r_k = 32, batch 32); plus the k_chunk overrides 128/512. PCC ≥ 0.99 against the
fp32 torch reference in `palu_tt.fused` and ≥ 0.995 against the stock MLA op fed host-rotated K, with
the worst observed case ≈ 0.9998. Attention level (`tests/test_attention_device.py`,
`test_fused_decode_matches_naive`): fused-vs-naive output PCC **0.99966–0.99996** over six decode
positions on both `svd_r64` and the mixed `svd_rk32_rv128`, and the K and V latent caches compare
**byte-identical** (`torch.equal`) on every combination — the fused path writes the caches through
exactly the stage-1 code and only changes what reads them.

**(2) "No slower than stock at 1k and 4k, and runs at 8k" — the 8k half is met, the parity half is
missed by 11–17 %.** Whole decode step, batch 32, minima:

| row | 1024 | 4096 | 8192 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `baseline` (stock) | **38.0** | **55.4** | OOM |
| `palu_full_50` naive | 88.5 (0.43x) | 265.3 (0.21x) | 488.5 |
| `palu_full_50_fk2` | **42.8 (0.89x)** | **65.0 (0.85x)** | **94.5** |
| `palu_full_70` naive | 90.2 (0.42x) | 262.8 (0.21x) | 481.5 |
| `palu_full_70_fk2` | **43.1 (0.88x)** | **66.4 (0.83x)** | **97.7** |

Both calibrated rows run at 8k, where stock does not allocate, at **94.5 / 97.7 ms** — 1.7–1.8x the
stock **4096** step for twice the context, and **4.9–5.2x faster than the same rows on the naive
path**. But at 1k and 4k they are still **0.83–0.89x stock**, i.e. 4.8 ms (1k) and 9.6–11.0 ms (4k)
per step slower — about 0.15 and 0.30 ms per layer. Stage 2 closes **95 % of the stage-1 excess** at 4k (the naive row was 209.9 ms over stock; the
fused row is 9.6 ms over) and does not close the last of it. Every other fused row behaves the same way:
`svd_r64_fk2` 0.84x / 0.80x, `svd_r96_fk2` 0.81x / 0.76x.

For context, the fastest 8k row in this study is still stage 1's `svd_rk128_rv64` (69.2 ms), which
does not compress K at all and therefore never reconstructs it; `palu_full_50_fk2` is 1.37x that
while storing 35.9 KiB/token against 51.0.

**(3) Decode fidelity, PCC ≥ 0.99 (min over 63 decode steps) and argmax agreement ≥ 0.95 —
PCC met everywhere, argmax agreement met on two rows of four.** `decode_check` runs one shared
prefill step plus 63 teacher-forced decode steps; the prefill logit is bit-identical between the
naive and fused paths (the fused kernel only replaces decode), so it is excluded and only the 63
decode steps are compared (final review: an earlier pass over all 64 steps inflated agreement by
1/64 by counting that bit-identical step as agreement).

| row | PCC min / mean | argmax agree | base-row ppl |
|---|---|---|---|
| `svd_r64_fk2` | 0.9972 / 0.9985 | **0.952** | 703.6 |
| `palu_full_50_fk1` | 0.9983 / 0.9994 | 0.937 | 12.931 |
| `palu_full_50_fk2` | 0.9980 / 0.9994 | 0.921 | 12.931 |
| `palu_full_70_fk2` | 0.9988 / 0.9993 | **0.984** | 9.028 |

The PCC bar passes with two orders of magnitude of margin on every row. The argmax bar fails on both
`palu_full_50` rows; `svd_r64_fk2` clears it, but only by 0.002. **The agreement number is not
measuring the kernel** — it inversely tracks how decisive the *model* is, not how accurate the *op*
is: `palu_full_50_fk2` has a **higher** logit PCC than `svd_r64_fk2` (0.9980 vs 0.9972) and a
**lower** agreement (0.921 vs 0.952), and the ordering by agreement (0.984 / 0.952 / 0.937 / 0.921) is
the ordering by how peaked each model's next-token distribution is, not by numerical error. At ratio
0.5 the model is degraded enough (ppl 12.9 against stock 7.25; its stage-1 samples drift into "a 3D 3D
3D …") that near-ties are common and a 1e-3 logit difference flips the top token. Stage 1 saw the same
effect on the same metric, against a completely different comparand: `svd_r64` scored 0.906
teacher-forced agreement against the fp32 CPU reference while stock scored 0.969. A 0.95 argmax bar
was the wrong threshold to write for a model this damaged; the PCC column is the one that says
whether the kernel is faithful, and it says yes.

## 2. What Phase 1 versus Phase 2 shows

Spec §4 predicts the whole result from K-side DRAM traffic per position: stock reads 128 elements of
K (bfp8, 136 B); **Phase 1** reads `r_k + 256` (the latent plus a streamed `[cos | sin]` row — 320
elements at r_k = 64, *worse than stock*); **Phase 2** reads `r_k` alone (64 elements, 68 B) because
the cos/sin table becomes chunk-relative, resident in L1, and the chunk base is carried by the query.
Phase 1 is a correctness checkpoint by construction. The attention-only column measures exactly this,
isolated from the rest of the step (`svd_r64`, minima, ms):

| context | stock | naive | Phase 1 | Phase 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1024 | 0.81 | 2.69 | 1.28 | 1.13 |
| 4096 | 1.31 | 8.22 | 2.51 | 1.97 |
| 8192 | 2.07 | 15.05 | 4.48 | 2.94 |
| **slope, ms per extra 1024 positions** | **0.18** | **1.77** | **0.46** | **0.26** |

Three things follow.

- **The fused op removes the reconstruction cost, and Phase 2 removes most of what Phase 1 leaves.**
  Against the naive path the attention block is **5.1x cheaper at 8k** (15.05 → 2.94 ms) and its
  growth with context is **6.8x flatter**. Phase 1 gets to 3.4x and a 3.9x flatter slope; Phase 2 is
  a further 1.5x on top of it at 8k. The ordering the spec predicted holds at every context.
- **Phase 1 is bandwidth-limited by the table it streams; Phase 2 is not bandwidth-limited at all.**
  Phase 1 reads 3x the bytes of Phase 2 per position (320 + 64 vs 64 + 64, K + V) and its slope is
  only **1.76x** Phase 2's, which is what a partly bandwidth-bound path looks like once the extra
  per-chunk overhead of its forced 128-row chunk is added. Phase 2, by contrast, reads **half** the
  bytes stock does (128 vs 256 elements per position) and its slope is still **1.44x** stock's. Its residual cost is
  therefore compute and per-chunk overhead, not DRAM: the on-core `AB = lat @ w_rot` matmul and the
  eight multiplies plus four SFPU adds per row tile, plus the Q-side work the op carries because
  `use_half_tile` is forced off (full 32-row Q tiles for 16 query heads per chip). That is also why
  the whole-step parity target is missed: there is no traffic left to save, only math.
- **The fused path's cost now tracks the latent size, which the naive path's never did.** At 8k,
  Phase 2 attention costs 2.94 ms at r_k = r_v = 64 and 3.67 ms at 96 — a 1.25x rise for 1.5x the
  bytes. The naive path at the same two ranks costs 15.05 and 15.72 ms, a 1.04x rise, because what it
  paid for was the full-width `[32, 4, S, 128]` reconstruction output and a RoPE pass over it, which
  is the same work at any rank. Compression now buys decode time, not just memory.

Whole-step numbers follow the attention column: Phase 2 beats Phase 1 by 6–9 % at 1k, 23–26 % at 4k
and 30–33 % at 8k (e.g. `palu_full_50` 46.7 / 87.5 / 141.9 ms in Phase 1 against 42.8 / 65.0 / 94.5 in
Phase 2).

## 3. Caveats on these numbers

- **Compute config.** Both phases resolved to the model's *stock* SDPA-decode compute config —
  `MathFidelity.HiFi2` with `fp32_dest_acc_en=True` — because that preset already carries fp32 dest
  accumulation, which is what spec §3 requires for Phase 2. The `compute_kernel_config_hifi4`
  fallback in `PaluAttention` was never taken, so the fused rows are compared against stock at
  **equal math fidelity**, and stage 1's open question about HiFi4 on the reconstruction matmuls is
  still open (the fused op does that matmul at HiFi2 too).
- **Phase 2's query stepping is effectively tf32.** The state and `rot_step` are fp32 CBs, but
  Wormhole's `mul_tiles` unpacks fp32 into 19-bit Src registers, so each per-chunk step carries ~11
  mantissa bits. Sixteen steps at 8k stay inside every PCC bar with margin (Task 4), but this is the
  one place in the op where precision depends on the number of chunks.
- **Chunk size: the defaults were used everywhere and `--fused-k-chunk` was never needed.** Phase 2
  ran at the op's own chunk (512 at 1k/4k/8k, ~990 KB of L1 per core at r_k = 96, inside Wormhole's
  1.5 MB); no fused row failed to allocate at any context, at batch 32, at 8k. Spec §9's risk 2 did
  not materialise, so **the stock sensitivity row at chunk 128 was not measured** — there is nothing
  for it to explain. Phase 1 is pinned to `k_chunk_size = 128` by the op's own validation (its
  streamed table needs 512 KB at chunk 512), so the Phase-1 rows carry a 4x-chunk-count overhead that
  Phase 2 does not; part of the P1-vs-P2 gap above is that, not just traffic.
- **`use_half_tile` is forced off** in the copied op, so the fused path does full-tile Q math where
  stock would use 16-row tiles for ≤ 16 query heads. That cost is inside every fused number here.
- **The attention-only bench builds layer 0 only**, and layer 0 of *both* calibrated dumps has the
  same ranks (32 / 128), so the `palu_full_50` and `palu_full_70` attention columns measure identical
  work — the same stage-1 caveat. Its run-to-run spread is large (stage 1 measured up to 2x at 1k),
  so `32 × attention-only` reproduces the whole-step number only to within a few ms, and no
  difference smaller than that should be read as a difference between rows.
- **Perplexity, prefill and the stage-1 rows are not re-measurements.** Perplexity here runs through
  prefill windows, which reconstruct K and V in flight and never enter the decode kernel, so a fused
  row's perplexity and TTFT are its base row's *by construction* — that is why the three rows of each
  group show identical prefill numbers, and it is exactly the blind spot `palu_tt.decode_check`
  exists to cover. The `baseline` and naive rows are the stage-1 measurements, unchanged.
- **Provenance.** All fourteen fused bench rows and all four decode checks were measured on an idle
  host on 2026-09-10 between 14:40 and 14:50, sequentially, one process at a time, each gated by
  `scripts/device_free.sh`; the two extra max-context probes ran 14:53–14:55. `p50` and `min` agree to
  within 1 % on every fused row (e.g. 43.0 / 42.8), which is what an uncontended untraced decode
  looks like — the stage-1 contention signature was a p50 far above the min. Nothing was
  re-measured. Every number above comes from a JSON in `docs/results/raw/`.

## 4. What did not work, and what was not measured

- **Stock parity at 1k and 4k was not reached** (0.83–0.89x). See §2: after Phase 2 there is no DRAM
  traffic left to remove — the fused op already reads half of stock's bytes — so the remaining
  10–15 % is on-core math and per-chunk overhead. The identified levers are `use_half_tile` (Q-side
  tiles), moving the per-chunk query step onto the SFPU, and two smaller ones found reading
  `palu_build_k_chunk` (`palu_compute_common.hpp`): per row tile it does `DHt` (= 4) full
  `tile_regs_acquire`/`commit`/`wait`/`release` round trips to pack one output tile each, when two `j`
  values fit in one acquisition at `dst_size = 4`; and it re-runs `matmul_block_init` plus
  `mul_init`/`add_binary_tile_init` (and two `reconfig_data_format` calls) on every row tile, of which
  only the format reconfigs actually need to happen per tile — the inits are hoistable out of the
  loop. None of the four levers was attempted.
- **Max context did not improve, but the reason it does not changed.** Stage 1 found that `svd_r64`
  and `palu_full_50` failed at 16k on an **8–9 MiB reconstruction scratch** (`[32, 4, 256, 128]` bf16)
  rather than on the cache, and predicted stage 2 would remove that limit. It does remove the
  scratch: re-probing both rows with the fused op (`bench_maxctx_svd_r64_fk2`,
  `bench_maxctx_palu_full_50_fk2`) shows the failing allocation moved from 9,191,424 / 8,388,608 B to
  **142,606,336 B** (exactly the rank-64 latent cache, `32 · 4 · 16384 · 64 · 1.0625`) and
  **31,752,192 B**. The ceiling is still 8192 at batch 32 — the caches and the model now bound it, as
  they already did for the other rows — so the capacity claim in stage 1 §2(c) is corrected here:
  removing the reconstruction buffers was necessary for 16k but not sufficient. (These two probes are
  an addition to the planned matrix; spec §8 assumed max context could not change.)
- **Not measured**: `svd_r96_fk1` (Phase 1 at rank 96 — the spec's own worst case for Phase 1, and
  the matrix omits it); the op-level r_k = 96 long-context case (S ∈ {4096, 8192}, Phase 2) — the
  op-test grid in §1(1) only exercises r_k = 96 at S = 1024, so the largest-latent, longest-context
  combination is covered empirically rather than directly, by the `svd_r96_fk2` 8k attention/decode
  bench rows in §2 rather than by an op-level PCC check; decode checks for `svd_r96`,
  `palu_full_70_fk1` and any Phase-1 row other than `palu_full_50_fk1`; a fused perplexity (it cannot
  differ; see above); traced decode, which
  would remove the host-dispatch component that untraced decode carries in every whole-step number
  here (stock's 38.0 ms step at 1k holds only 32 x 0.81 = 25.8 ms of attention) and would change the
  stock-parity ratio in an unknown direction; paged caches; and generation samples for the fused
  rows.
- **The argmax-agreement criterion as written is not a useful acceptance test** for a model at ratio
  0.5, for the reason given in §1(3). It is reported rather than quietly dropped, and the PCC column
  beside it is what should be read as the fidelity result.

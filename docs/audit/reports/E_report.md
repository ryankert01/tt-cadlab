## Findings

### [Critical] The report and figure R2 state the naive path's attention op costs 8 ms at 4k; stage 2d measures 22.12 ms

File: `docs/report_palu_on_tt.md:73`; `scripts/report_figures.py:190-192` →
`docs/figures/report_r2_vanilla_path.svg:66` area.

What. §4 reads: "The stage 2d profile put numbers on why: at 4k the reconstruction block costs
207 ms of the 265 ms step (matmul 91, RoPE 80, typecast 33) **while the attention op itself is
8 ms**." The R2 cost panel, explicitly labelled "(batch 32, context 4k, per decode step; stage 2d
profile)", repeats it: `"  the attention op itself               8 ms"`. `docs/results/stage2d.md:86`
gives, for exactly this row and context, `| palu_full_50 | 4096 | 259.61 | **22.12** | 206.74 |
30.75 |`, and `stage2d.md:236` states it in words: "The naive rows' *attention op* is meanwhile
cheaper than stock — **22.12** vs 24.24 ms at 4k".

Why it matters. Every other number in that sentence is whole-model at 4k (207, 265, 91, 80, 33),
so the reader reads 8 ms as whole-model too. The 8 ms is almost certainly
`bench_attention_palu_full_50.json` at 4096 (min 8.307 ms), which is a **layer-0-only** build — one
thirty-second of the model, and one that still includes the reconstruction. The error understates
the naive attention op by 2.8x and, worse, it makes the rhetorical point ("207 ms of rebuild to feed
an 8 ms op") 2.8x stronger than the data supports; stage 2d's own framing is "7.2–9.6x the cost of
the attention op it is feeding", i.e. 9.35x at 4k, not 26x.

How to verify. `python3 -c "import json; d=json.load(open('docs/results/raw/bench_attention_palu_full_50.json')); print(d['data']['4096'])"`
gives `min_ms` 8.307 (layer 0 only — see `stage1.md`: "The attention-only bench builds **layer 0
only** (`args.n_layers = 1`)"). Compare `docs/results/stage2d.md:86`.

Suggested fix. Change both to 22 ms (or "0.69 ms per layer"), and re-run
`python scripts/report_figures.py` so the committed SVG follows.

### [Important] The 2f frontier's `decode 4k (fused)` column silently mixes kernel variants, and misses the shipped group-shared bfp8 G-LRD file

File: `palu_tt/report.py:1310-1332` (`FRONTIER_FUSED_SUFFIXES`, `_frontier_fused_bench`),
`palu_tt/report.py:1373-1375` (`decode_4k_suffix` computed), `palu_tt/report.py:1420-1430`
(suffix never rendered); `docs/results/stage2f.md` table rows `glrd_50_f256` and
`glrd_50_f256_l4`.

What. The lookup order is `_fk2dm`, `_fk2gdm`, `_fk2`. For `glrd_50_f256` only
`bench_decode_glrd_50_f256_fk2.json` matches, so the table shows **105.8 ms** — the *legacy,
per-head* (`group_heads: 1`, `rot_mode: null`) kernel. But the same dump *was* measured on the
shipped group-shared dst+multicast kernel: `bench_decode_glrd_50_fk2gdm.json` carries
`"palu_dir": "compressed/glrd_0.5_f256"`, `group_heads: auto`, `rot_mode: dst`, `table_mcast: on`,
and **95.5 ms** at 4k. The file's row name (`glrd_50_fk2gdm`) does not contain `_f256`, so
`_frontier_fused_bench` cannot find it. Its bfp4 sibling `glrd_50_f256_l4` *does* resolve to
`_fk2gdm` (93.7 ms).

Why it matters. In the frontier table the bfp8 G-LRD-0.5 row (105.8) sits ten rows from its bfp4
sibling (93.7), inviting the reading "bfp4 is 11 % faster". The same-kernel comparison — which the
hand-written §"Format-independence" makes by hand, bypassing the table — is **1.9 %**. The same
applies to `glrd_70_f256` (121.5, legacy per-head) vs `glrd_70_f256_l4` (109.2, shipped). The code
computes `decode_4k_suffix`/`maxctx_suffix` precisely to disambiguate this and then never renders
either; the generated header text also only names `_fk2dm` and `_fk2`, omitting `_fk2gdm`. The
`palu_full_50` row compounds it: its decode comes from `_fk2dm` and its max-context from `_fk2`.

How to verify.
`python3 -c "import json;print(json.load(open('docs/results/raw/bench_decode_glrd_50_fk2gdm.json'))['palu_dir'])"`
→ `compressed/glrd_0.5_f256`; compare 95.5 against the 105.8 in the committed `stage2f.md` row.

Suggested fix. Either rename the two mis-named artefacts (`bench_decode_glrd_50_fk2gdm`,
`decode_check_glrd_50_fk2gdm` → `..._glrd_50_f256_fk2gdm`) or add the row-name alias; and render
`decode_4k_suffix` in the cell (e.g. `95.5 (fk2gdm)`), since it is already carried.

### [Important] "within a quarter point of the paper's own G-LRD number" compares our 5-task mean against the paper's 6-task mean

File: `docs/report_palu_on_tt.md:77` ("What it costs in quality" paragraph).

What. The claim: "G-LRD at 0.7 with a 256-sample calibration reaches perplexity 8.14 and 64.5 on
the tasks, within a point of the baseline on the tasks and **within a quarter point of the paper's
own G-LRD number**." Our `glrd_70_f256` five-task plain-`acc` mean is 64.533. The only paper number
within a quarter point of it is `PAPER["G-LRD"]["avg6"] = 64.45` — the paper's **six**-task average
(`report.py:196`). The comparable five-task figure, computed by report.py itself, is
`PAPER["G-LRD"]["avg5"] = 63.27` — a 1.26-point gap. The generated stage-2b text
(`report.py:855-858`) says in so many words: "we evaluate five ... so `avg (5)` above, **not
`avg (6)`**, is the comparable column."

Why it matters. This is the study's headline "we match the paper on G-LRD" claim in the
externally-facing report, and it rests on a coincidental collision between two differently-defined
averages. Two further mismatches ride on it: the paper's G-LRD row is at **50 %** compression while
ours is ratio 0.7 (1.43x, i.e. less compression), and the paper's per-task values are acc_norm for
OBQA/ARC-c (see next finding) while 64.5 is plain `acc`.

How to verify. `PAPER["G-LRD"]` in `palu_tt/report.py:193-195` plus the derived `avg5` at
`report.py:199-202`; our 64.533 from `docs/results/raw/tasks_glrd_70_f256.json`.

Suggested fix. Quote the comparable pair ("64.5 against the paper's five-task 63.3, at a milder
compression ratio"), or use the paper-protocol average both sides
(`glrd_70_f256` 67.03 vs paper 63.27) and say so.

### [Important] `_kv_model_mean` rescales by the rank *sum*, which is wrong for any mixed-format row

File: `palu_tt/report.py:322-344`.

What. It takes `bench`'s layer-0 `kv_bytes_per_token_per_layer_per_chip` and multiplies by
`mean(r_k+r_v) / (r_k[0]+r_v[0])`. That is exact only when K and V have the same bytes/element.
For a `_lk4`/`_lv4` row (K bfp8, V bfp4 or vice versa) the two halves scale differently.
Concretely, `bench_decode_palu_full_70_lv4_fk2dm.json` records layer-0 424.0 B
(`4*(32*1.0625 + 128*0.5625)`); the helper would return `424 * 179/160 = 474.4`, while the correct
model mean is `4*(58*1.0625 + 121*0.5625) = 518.75`. 9 % low.

Why it matters. Today it is latent: the only mixed-format rows are reported through
`_stage2f_lines`, which uses the independent (and correct) `_dump_ranks`/`BPE` formula, so the
published 518.8 B / 32.42 KB / 2.10x figures for the study's *recommended configuration* are right.
But `_kv_model_mean` is what `_quality_row` (stages 1/2/2b) and the stage-2e L4 table use, and the
raw dir already contains `_lv4` bench files. The moment an `_lv4` row is added to `ROWS2`/`ROWS2E`
the KV column silently goes wrong, and no test covers it.

How to verify. `report._kv_model_mean(424.0, [json.load(open('docs/results/raw/bench_decode_palu_full_70_lv4_fk2dm.json'))])`
against `4*(58*1.0625+121*0.5625)`.

Suggested fix. Rescale K and V separately (the helper already loads the per-layer rank lists), or
drop the rescale and compute bytes from ranks and formats the way `frontier_rows` does, so there is
one byte formula in the codebase instead of two.

### [Important] Nothing ties the committed stage documents to the raw JSONs

### [Important] `--stage 2f` has no "no data" guard, so a mistyped `--raw` blanks the committed 2f table

File: `palu_tt/report.py:578-586` (the early `return _finish(_stage2f_lines(raw), out)` that skips

## Tests in this area

- **Real raw files**: none of the 50 tests read `docs/results/raw`. The only test that touches a
  committed artefact for its *content* is `test_paper_reference_matches_the_spec_table`, which
  parses the stage-2b spec markdown and checks `report.PAPER` cell for cell (a genuine independent
  source, and a good test). `test_report_references_every_report_figure` checks link presence only.
- **Synthetic fixtures**: everything else. They are well built — hermetic (`monkeypatch.chdir` +
  `_palu_recipe_dir` where a real `compressed/` dump would otherwise be read), they assert against
  *independently computed* expectations rather than recorded output (e.g. "the KV cell is the model
  mean, not layer 0's 680 (layer 0 sums to 160, the mean to 128)" → 544), and several encode a
  specific past defect with the wrong answer spelled out (`test_frontier_rows_bytes_use_the_dumps_own_groups_per_chip`
  names the old 2180.25 B). `test_main_refuses_to_render_when_no_row_has_any_data` asserts the
  output file is byte-unchanged after the refusal. I found no assertion that cannot fail and no
  tautological assertion.
- **Would a wrong bytes formula be caught?** Partly. The 2f formula
  (`_dump_ranks` × `BPE` × `groups_per_chip`) is well covered: `test_frontier_rows_parse_the_2f_row_names`,
  `..._bytes_use_the_dumps_own_groups_per_chip`, `test_config_groups_per_layer_...`,
  `..._parse_and_read_a_split_budget_dump` all recompute the expected bytes from first principles
  including the mixed `_lv4` case. The *other* byte path, `_kv_model_mean`, is covered only for the
  uniform-format case (`test_ranks_from_head_wise_ranks_...` asserts 544), which is exactly why the
  mixed-format defect above survives.
- **Would a wrong paper-protocol average be caught?** Yes — `test_avg5_paper_matches_the_papers_protocol`
  pins 62.18 from an explicit fixture and checks the two "missing field" paths, and
  `test_stage2c_calibration_table_...` checks the rendered cell and the `+1.42` delta. **The plain

## Verdict

The generator is in good shape: all six stage documents regenerate byte-identically from the
committed raw JSONs, the byte/rank/ratio/average/slope/Pareto formulas all reproduce independently,
and the hand-written analyses in stage 1, 2, 2b, 2c, 2e and 2f are unusually accurate — I checked
roughly 120 individual figures in them and found only rounding-level slips. The problems are
concentrated in the two places that are *not* generated: `docs/report_palu_on_tt.md` and
`scripts/report_figures.py`, where every number is a hand-transcribed literal guarded only by a
self-snapshot test — one of them (the naive path's attention op at 4k, stated as 8 ms against a
measured 22.12 ms) is simply wrong, and three summary ranges quietly exclude their own worst
measurement. The two structural risks worth fixing before publication are the 2f frontier's
`decode 4k (fused)` column, which mixes kernel variants across rows and makes bfp4 look ~11 %
faster than the same-kernel 1.9 %, and the absence of any test tying the committed documents to the
raw JSONs — today they are in sync, but nothing keeps them that way.
